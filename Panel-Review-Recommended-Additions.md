# Panel Review — What Else to Add to the Wealth Guide

> **Prepared:** June 9, 2026 · A four-expert review of *Wealth-Through-Real-Estate-and-Leverage.md*
> **The panel:** (1) CPA / tax strategist, (2) hands-on rental operator, (3) asset-protection attorney / risk manager, (4) fee-only financial planner (CFP). Each reviewed the guide independently, then a chair consolidated and cross-examined their notes.
>
> **Note:** any section numbers (§) referenced below point to the *pre-v3* layout. All of these items were implemented in the guide's v3 rewrite, which renumbered everything into 7 parts / 30 sections. This file is kept as a record of the review.

---

## The headline verdict

**Your guide is a 9/10 wealth-*building* manual sitting on a ~4/10 wealth-*protection* foundation.** All four reviewers independently said a version of the same thing: the offense is excellent and current (tax deferral, leverage, entity structures, estate planning), but the defense is thin and in a few places too optimistic. It's mostly accelerator, not much brake.

**The fix is structural, not a rewrite:** keep every aggressive tactic, but add a *risk budget at the front* (goals + concentration ceiling) and a *stress test + insurance at the back*. A barbell — guardrails on both ends, engine in the middle. Nothing gets removed; the advanced material is what makes the guide valuable. It just gets bounded.

---

## Consensus items (the strongest signal — each raised by 2+ reviewers)

These are the highest-priority because multiple independent experts flagged them.

**1. Insurance — "the single biggest hole."** *(Operator, Risk, Planner)*
Right now insurance appears only as an expense line. Missing: landlord/dwelling (DP-3) policies, per-property liability, a **personal umbrella ($1–5M)**, **flood and earthquake** (both *excluded* from standard policies — flood needs separate NFIP/private coverage), **loss-of-rent** coverage, **builder's-risk** for vacant properties during BRRRR rehabs (a normal landlord policy often voids coverage on a vacant/under-renovation home), and requiring tenants to carry renters insurance. Insurance — not the LLC — is your *first and primary* layer of asset protection.

**2. A "should I, and how much?" front gate.** *(Planner, with Risk on concentration)*
The guide teaches how to go fast but never asks how fast you *should* go. Add: a target number (cash-flow and/or net-worth goal + time horizon), a hard **concentration ceiling** (a cap on real-estate-plus-leverage as a share of total net worth, counting your home equity), and a **sequencing waterfall** (high-interest debt → employer 401k match → reserves funded → baseline index investing → first/next deal). This is the brake that makes everything after it safe to use.

**3. A real multi-shock stress test.** *(Risk, Planner)*
The current §15 checklist tests one bad thing at a time. Real downturns are *correlated* — values fall AND rents soften AND a refi comes due at a higher rate AND the SBLOC margin-calls AND a tenant stops paying, all at once. Add a scenario that runs them simultaneously, plus **balloon/maturity rollover risk** (what happens when a portfolio or DSCR loan matures into a frozen credit market).

**4. The short-term-rental (STR) tax loophole.** *(CPA, Operator — the only item both flagged)*
A short-term rental where the **average guest stay is ≤7 days** is not a "rental activity" under the passive-loss rules. If you **materially participate**, the losses are non-passive and **offset your W-2 income** — with no 750-hour real-estate-professional requirement. This is the most accessible way for a high-income W-2 investor to actually use the cost-segregation + 100% bonus depreciation the guide already teaches. Its absence is the biggest hole in the otherwise tax-savvy sections.

**5. The limits of the LLC + personal-guarantee reality.** *(Risk, CPA)*
The guide builds an elaborate entity fortress but never teaches the discipline that keeps it standing or the fact that it often doesn't protect you. Add: **veil-piercing hygiene** (separate bank account + EIN per entity, no commingling, adequate capitalization, a real signed operating agreement, signing contracts "as Manager"), the note that **single-member LLC charging-order protection is weak/untested** in many states, and — most important — that a **personal guarantee makes the LLC shield irrelevant** for most of your debt (plus deficiency judgments, "bad-boy" carve-outs, and cancellation-of-debt income on a foreclosure/short sale).

**6. Bookkeeping & records hygiene.** *(CPA, Operator)*
The same habit pays off twice: separate accounts and clean books per entity keep the liability silos intact *and* make your tax deductions defensible. Add: per-property **basis & depreciation schedules** kept for the life of the asset (sloppy records cause recapture on depreciation you never even claimed), land/building allocation method, and software (Stessa, Baselane, DoorLoop).

**7. Honesty edit on "buy, borrow, die" and SBLOC.** *(Risk, Planner)*
Not a new section — a correction. The §6 and §13 framing oversells it. Borrowing against your stock portfolio to buy real estate is **double leverage on two correlated assets** — in a 2008-style event both legs fall together (margin call forces a taxable sale at the bottom *while* your real estate can't refinance). Cap it hard, label it bridge-only, and sharpen the caveat that the whole strategy depends on never being a forced seller — and on the step-up-at-death rule surviving (a recurring reform target).

## The one real tension — and how it resolves

The **planner** says the guide is too aggressive and wants brakes. The **operator and CPA** want *more* advanced tactics (which add leverage). That sounds contradictory but isn't — it's a missing frame. The guide teaches "how to go fast" with no "how fast should you go" layer.

**Resolution:** keep all the aggressive tactics, but bound them. Risk budget at the front (goals + concentration cap), stress test + insurance at the back, honesty edit on the crown-jewel chapters. Aggressive engine, explicit guardrails. Nobody loses content.

## How it would slot into your existing guide

**Four new sections; everything else folds into what's already there.**

New sections:
- **A front-matter "Set Your Risk Budget" section** (before Core Idea) — goals, concentration ceiling, sequencing waterfall, RE-vs-passive opportunity cost. The brake, placed first so it gates everything.
- **An "Insurance" section** — currently has no home and is too important to bury.
- **A "Finding Deals & Building Your Team" section** — the guide jumps to evaluating a deal that's already in front of you.
- **An "Operating & Legal Compliance" section** — tenant screening, landlord law, due diligence, market & property-class selection.

Folded into existing sections: the stress test upgrades §15; LLC-limits/guarantees go into §10/§12; the STR loophole, NIIT, quarterly taxes, bookkeeping, Augusta, and safe harbors go into §9; the SBLOC honesty edit touches §6 and §13; house hacking goes into §2/§5; continuity planning into §13 + the new Insurance section.

**Resulting flow:** risk budget → core idea → sourcing & team → evaluating → operating & legal → financing → insurance → the engine (portfolio borrowing, BRRRR, equity, tax, entity, estate — with the honest SBLOC reframe) → stress test → your numbers.

## Bottom line

Build the four new sections, upgrade the risk checklist into a real stress test, and run an honesty pass on the buy-borrow-die / SBLOC material. That absorbs every Tier 1 item and resolves the core tension. The guide stays ambitious — it just stops being all-accelerator. **That balance is the single most important change the panel recommends.**
