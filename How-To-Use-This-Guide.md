# How To Use This Guide
### The operating system — so the knowledge actually gets applied and stays fresh

> This page is the "how," not the "what." The *what* is in **Wealth-Through-Real-Estate-and-Leverage.md**; the interactive tools are in **Guide-Companion.html** (open it in any browser). This page ties them together into a routine grounded in what actually works.

---

## The one idea to take seriously

The research on learning is unusually blunt: **re-reading and highlighting are among the *weakest* ways to absorb a guide** — they create a feeling of fluency that fades. What actually builds usable, decision-ready knowledge is the opposite of passive review:

1. **Retrieval, not review.** Quizzing yourself (recalling from memory) beats re-reading, by a lot — especially questions framed as *"you're facing X, what do you do?"* That trains the exact skill you want: the right concept surfacing at the moment of a decision.
2. **Memorize the map, look up the math.** You don't need the formulas in your head. You need to *know a tool exists and when it applies* (the triggers), then look up the precise details from one canonical document at the point of need.
3. **Decision rules + checklists fire the knowledge.** Knowing something and retrieving it under pressure are different skills. Short checklists and if-then rules ("if DSCR < my floor at the stress rate → pass") bridge that gap.
4. **A decision journal makes you better over time.** Logging each call *at the time* — and reviewing it later — is how judgment compounds instead of repeating the same mistakes.

Everything below operationalizes those four.

## The three layers (engage at the right depth)

You shouldn't read 30 sections every time a deal lands. Use the guide in layers:

- **Layer 1 — the cheat sheet** (the home tab of *Guide-Companion.html*): your red-lines, the "if you're facing X → go to §Y" router, and the 6 core ideas. This is what you actually open in the moment.
- **Layer 2 — the full guide** (*…and-Leverage.md*): the reasoning, edge cases, and worked detail behind each cheat-sheet line.
- **Layer 3 — the sources** (the Sources section): IRS pages, statutes, calculators — for when you need to verify a number or go deep.

Glance at Layer 1; drop to Layer 2 when it matters; hit Layer 3 to verify.

## The rhythm (a realistic cadence)

You don't need to study hard. You need a light, repeating loop.

**First pass (weeks 1–4).** Read the guide one or two sections at a time, spread across days — not in one binge (spacing beats cramming). After each section, close it and spend two minutes recalling the gist from memory. Then open the companion's **Self-Quiz** and rate the cards for that topic.

**Ongoing retention (a few times a week, ~10 min).** Run the **Self-Quiz**. Answer *before* flipping; rate honestly. Cards you miss come back sooner. As you learn, this naturally tapers — you'll spend less time as more cards become "got it."

**Every deal (the application loop).** When a deal lands, open the **Deal Calculator** and the **Deal-screening checklist**. Every real decision is the highest-value practice there is — and it ends with a **Decision Journal** entry.

**Quarterly + tax season (30–60 min refresh).** Re-explain the 3–4 highest-stakes sections to yourself in plain language (the gaps you stumble on are what to re-study), run the **Annual tax & entity review** checklist, and update any dated figures in the guide (rates, brackets, limits). Bump the changelog.

> **Make it automatic.** Put the quarterly + tax-season refresh on your calendar as a recurring event. Attach the deal tools to a trigger: *"If I save a deal to my pipeline, then I open the screening checklist first."* Willpower is unreliable; cues and calendar events aren't.

## Keep it a living document (without it becoming a chore)

Two things kill guides: **dated figures quietly going stale**, and **the update never happening.** Defenses:

- **Quarantine the volatile numbers.** Anything dated (rates, tax brackets, loan limits, the ⏱️ items) is the first thing to re-check each quarter — they're already flagged ⏱️ throughout the guide.
- **The changelog is the trust signal.** If it was touched recently, rely on it; if it's untouched for a year, verify before acting. Add a line every time you change something.
- **"Update is part of done."** A deal isn't finished until you've logged one lesson in the journal. Recurring lessons get promoted into the relevant guide section.
- **One canonical copy.** Don't fork the guide into stale duplicates. Edit the one file; let everything else (companion app, notes) point at it.

## Where it all lives (a minimal, no-over-engineering stack)

You already have the two files. To make them durable, searchable, and usable on your phone:

**Obsidian (free) — the home for the document.**
Your guide is already a markdown file, which is exactly Obsidian's native format. Point a new Obsidian "vault" at this folder and the file is unchanged on disk — you just gain instant search, a clickable outline, internal links, and a mobile app. No import, no lock-in. This is the recommended home; skip the elaborate "second brain" systems — for one guide they're overkill. Borrow only the one habit that matters: revisit and date your edits (the changelog).

**NotebookLM (free, Google) — "chat with your guide" + auto-quizzes.**
Upload a copy of the guide and you can ask it questions in plain language and get answers cited back to the exact section — great standing in a property on your phone (there's a mobile app). It can also auto-generate quizzes and flashcards if you want more than the companion's deck.

**The companion app — retrieval, checklists, calculator, journal.**
*Guide-Companion.html* already covers the active-use layer. It saves your progress in your browser; use the journal's **Export** button periodically so your entries also live as a file.

**A deal spreadsheet (optional).** For tracking your portfolio's actual numbers over time, a simple spreadsheet (or a free real-estate deal-analysis template) complements the calculator's per-deal math.

> ⚠️ **Privacy — read before uploading anything.** Your guide and journal will hold personal financial figures. Keep account numbers and identifiers out of anything you upload to a cloud AI. For the most sensitive "your numbers," prefer tools that stay on your device (the companion app is local-only; Obsidian files stay on your machine). Cloud AI tools (NotebookLM, Claude/ChatGPT projects) are convenient and their paid tiers generally don't train on your data — but verify each tool's current settings before trusting it with sensitive numbers, since these policies change.

## The 15-minute setup

1. Open **Guide-Companion.html** in your browser → go to **My Numbers** → set your target, concentration ceiling, minimum cash-on-cash, minimum DSCR, and reserve floor. (These now drive the cheat sheet and the calculator's verdicts.)
2. Do one round of the **Self-Quiz** to see where you stand.
3. (Optional) Install **Obsidian**, create a vault on this folder, and install its free *Spaced Repetition* plugin if you want reviews inside your notes; and/or upload the guide to **NotebookLM** for phone access.
4. Put two recurring events on your calendar: a **quarterly refresh** and a **tax-season review**.
5. From now on: new deal → calculator + checklist → journal entry. That's the whole system.
